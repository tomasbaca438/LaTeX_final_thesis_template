\section*{Theses and Objectives of the \typeofwork{} Thesis}

\normalsize

\subsection*{Theses}

Theses are concise and clear foundational statements that define what the thesis addresses, what problem it solves, what context it operates in, and what the main ideas or approaches are. Theses therefore represent the conceptual foundations of the work.

Theses describe the context, the problem, the methodological approach, or the theoretical assertions of the thesis.
They are not measurable; rather, they express “what is claimed / what the work is based on.”

Example theses:

\begin{itemize}
	\item Securing communication in computer networks is a key element in protecting sensitive data.
	\item Modern authentication protocols enable centralized management of user access rights.
	\item Implementing security mechanisms in enterprise networks reduces the risk of unauthorized access.
	\item Analysis of real network scenarios provides a practical view of the effectiveness of individual security solutions.
\end{itemize}


\subsection*{Objectives}

Objectives are specific measurable outputs that the thesis aims to achieve. They describe what we intend to demonstrate, create, analyze, and what results the thesis should deliver. Objectives therefore represent the concrete tasks carried out within the work.

Objectives are specific tasks that must be fulfilled. They are measurable, verifiable, and precisely define what the outcome of the thesis will be.

Example objectives:

\begin{itemize}
	\item Analyze available authentication protocols used in enterprise networks.
	\item Design and implement a client authentication model using a RADIUS server.
	\item Test the proposed solution in both simulated and real network environments.
	\item Evaluate the security benefits and limitations of the implemented solution.
\end{itemize}
